# Eye tracking V2

Open `/trial/screen-eye-tracking` for the six-step eye-tracking workspace. Camera capture, pupil detection, eye-model fitting, calibration, and gaze estimation run in the browser and do not require sign-in or the backend. `/v2` redirects to `/trial/screen-eye-tracking` for existing links. The separate backend remains for account authentication and API-key management.

## Run

From the repository root:

```sh
cd frontend
bun install --frozen-lockfile
bun run dev
```

Visit `http://localhost:4001/trial/screen-eye-tracking`. Camera capture requires localhost or HTTPS and browser camera permission. Prefer a close, steady view of one eye. A normal webcam may not resolve the pupil well enough. USB cameras and network streams are supported; frames are processed in a Web Worker and are not uploaded. Network streams use the local camera relay on port 4022. `bun run dev` starts the relay automatically; built previews and hosted apps need `bun run camera-relay` from `frontend/apps/web` separately. The relay supplies readable pixels without requiring camera-side CORS changes. Tracking still runs in the browser, independently of the auth backend.

The relay preserves the entered local-camera host, port, and path, including `.local` and private-IP streams. It does not silently change the endpoint to port 81. Name resolution happens on the relay host; the camera must be reachable from that machine. URLs and camera orientation preferences are remembered locally. The relay binds to loopback and limits accepted app origins and upstream destinations; see the scene-camera guide for hosted-origin configuration.

## Use the pipeline

1. **Camera:** choose a USB camera or enter a network stream URL. Camera names are listed when available and may appear after permission is granted. Permission is requested only when you start the preview.
2. **Eye region:** use **Edit ROI** to move the box, resize its eight handles, or **Redraw** it. Arrow keys move the region; Shift + arrows resize it and Alt uses 10-pixel steps. Numeric coordinates are applied together. Keep the full pupil movement inside the region, then continue; no confirmation checkbox is required. This is a manual region selection, not face or eye classification.
3. **Eye model:** inspect the pupil outline and threshold preview while looking around; threshold controls remain available above the preview. Dragging the slider updates segmentation immediately; numeric entries apply on Enter or blur. **Auto tracker** follows the last pupil rim and uses threshold segmentation when it needs a new outline; its slider adjusts the segmentation bias. **Manual tracker** applies one absolute grayscale cutoff (0–255), with an exact numeric entry. The threshold view shows the segmentation mask or the observed rim points used for tracking. A fit score measures geometric and image support, not the probability that the object is an eye. In Manual tracker, select the two eye corners. In Auto tracker, look in several directions until the blue sphere is stable, then lock it. Camera geometry is available under the disclosure.
4. **Head tracker (optional):** for a head-mounted eye camera, connect a separate front camera facing your face. Wait for one face to be detected, then continue. **Skip head tracking** stops that camera and continues with normal eye-only calibration. Entering this step never opens a camera automatically.
5. **Calibrate:** press **Start calibration** and look at each small spinning target until it pops. The stable center establishes the baseline and its noise. Each corner then requires movement in both matching directions; each edge requires the matching direction with the other axis near center. The initial inward-facing-camera convention mirrors horizontal motion. **Eye camera orientation** allows reversing either axis explicitly; orientation is never guessed from an arbitrary fixation. After the center and corners, the initial mapping also checks proximity at the edge targets. Eye-only mode uses nine dots. Head mode first collects the grid with the head near its starting pose, then collects twelve center fixations at different head positions: both directions of sideways/vertical/depth movement and pitch/yaw/roll. Keep looking at the center and hold each requested position briefly. The first direction of each movement is accepted on either side. Losing fresh eye or face readings, changing direction, or moving the estimated screen gaze resets the current dwell. In eye-only mode, keep your head steady.
6. **Live gaze:** open the gaze view, validate against five other targets, or export the current result as JSON. Validation reports root-mean-square distance in browser CSS pixels. It is not an angular-accuracy measurement.

Changing the source, format, crop, threshold, corners or camera geometry resets dependent estimates. Resizing the window invalidates screen calibration. Rebuild and recalibrate after moving the eye camera relative to your eye. Lost or stale pupils produce no current gaze instead of retaining the previous point. ROI, manual corners and threshold preferences persist separately for each tracker; screen calibration and head compensation last only for the current session.

## Optional head compensation

The front camera runs MediaPipe Face Landmarker in a separate worker, with one frame in flight and a requested maximum capture rate of 30 Hz. Device or inference speed may reduce this rate. The orientation toolbar applies quarter turns and mirrors before inference and to the preview. These settings persist locally; changing them clears calibration and expires old poses. Two visible faces, invalid non-rigid matrices, camera loss and worker errors suspend compensation. Disconnecting the eye source releases the front camera.

Head compensation now learns a projective eye-feature-to-head-ray map, the head-local eye origin, and the screen's center, rotation and width together. It rotates the fitted local ray and eye offset by the measured head rotation, adds head translation, and intersects that ray with the fitted screen plane. Screen aspect comes from the calibration viewport. Distances are expressed relative to the initial estimated face depth; they are not measured centimetres. This replaces the previous assumptions that raw features were already physical slopes, the face center was the eye origin, and the display was at `z=0`.

The nine grid targets seed the fit; they cannot identify compensation alone. The twelve movement fixations supply the additional geometry constraints. Calibration requires measured spans in all six pose components, a full-rank 18-parameter Jacobian, a low fitting error and whole-fixation cross-validation over every grid and movement target. Repeated exposures do not substitute for different poses. Each fixation contributes equal total weight. Fitting runs in another local worker and is cancellable. Insufficient or inconsistent data is rejected instead of returning a nominal compensated model. These gates establish numerical consistency, not commercial accuracy.

Eye and head camera frames use browser capture timestamps where available, otherwise video presentation timestamps or the frame-read time. A bounded history pairs eye frames with interpolated, bracketing head observations: translation is linear and rotation uses quaternion interpolation. Brackets over 80 ms, rotation jumps over 0.35 radians and readings older than 350 ms are rejected. A direct un-interpolated pair must be within 25 ms. No pose is extrapolated. MJPEG exposes no shared camera exposure timestamp, so its unknown network/decoder delay remains a dynamic-accuracy limitation.

Runtime uses the same fitted projection as calibration and validation, before averaging paired readings. Output pauses outside the observed axis ranges with a small numerical margin, on camera loss, or when a ray cannot intersect the display forward. Calibration targets and OpenGaze both use the full browser viewport, with no second mirror or card offset. Changing viewport size requires recalibration. Preview mirroring is not applied to the resulting screen point.

The initial grid requires a near-neutral head because the mounting geometry is still unknown. Movement samples are labelled by the displayed center target and the user's instruction compliance, not by an already calibrated attention detector. Eye stability cannot prove that someone is looking at an uncalibrated target. Independent five-dot validation deliberately does not gate capture by predicted proximity. Unknown live gaze is never used as new training labels.

MediaPipe's canonical face transform is approximate and uses a virtual camera. This code does not independently measure lens intrinsics/distortion, metric head pose, screen placement or headset slippage. The fitted geometry can absorb fixed offsets within its model, but cannot turn inaccurate face pose or unsynchronized exposures into ground truth. Accuracy across large motion, users, lighting, mounting changes and devices remains unverified. For research rationale, equations and the physical validation needed before sale, see [Head compensation research and implementation](head-compensation-research.md).

Face frames remain in the browser. The optional feature downloads a pinned MediaPipe WASM runtime and face model from public CDN/model hosts; no eye or face frames are uploaded. The [MediaPipe web guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker/web_js) describes outputs and worker guidance. Google's [face geometry documentation](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/docs/solutions/face_mesh.md#face-transform-module) explains its canonical coordinate space and virtual camera assumptions.

## What the two formats do

**Eye Tracker 1** reuses the existing `frontend/packages/ui/src/lib/gaze-core` grayscale conversion, equalization, detector and `gazeVector3D` implementation. Its manually selected corner midpoint and half corner distance define the original eye model. The V2 adapter adds source handling, invalid-frame gating and the shared previews and calibration. It adapts the original positive-z gaze to the V2 camera convention; this format does not reconstruct metric 3D pupil positions.

**Eye Tracker 2** implements the sequence from Jason Orlosky's [The Hidden Math Behind 3D Eye Tracking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gh8LS9erugE) and [MIT-licensed reference implementation](https://github.com/JEOresearch/EyeTracker/tree/main/3DTracker):

- Locate a dark, spatially uniform patch with a sparse mean/variance search and calculate an Otsu intensity split.
- With a recent accepted pupil, first measure dark-to-light edges along 64 normals in a narrow band around its last outline. Fit the current points and trim outliers. A brief partial fit can retain the last measured axes and angle for 200 ms. Sustained occlusion of a confirmed pupil uses a constrained fit of center and common scale from current arcs, retaining the unobserved aspect ratio and angle. It requires at least 20 identified inliers, a two-pixel residual bound, and a minimum normal-matrix eigenvalue ratio of 0.2; a one-direction lid edge cannot establish a center. Neither path outputs a measurement solely from history, and inferred shapes do not train the 3D eye model.
- For sustained partial tracking, remember the glint-excluded 20th- and 35th-percentile interior intensities of accepted full fits with confidence at least 0.8. Current rim edges must have a shallow interior no more than 32 gray levels above the 35th percentile, a deeper dark sample no more than 16 levels above it, and signed outward contrast. Several inward depths tolerate a glint crossing one path. Without learned intensities, the dark-patch mean provides a conservative fallback. Check identity before choosing the strongest normal-profile peak so a rejected reflection cannot hide a weaker true pupil edge. Inspect the exterior path from twice the local probe distance to 60% of the minor semiaxis, capped at 32 pixels, in one-pixel steps. A substantial brightness drop into the learned pupil range rejects an internal reflection; brighter glints and darker unrelated obstructions are skipped. Classify raw pixels into that range before interpolating membership, since interpolated lash/iris gray can resemble pupil gray even when neither pixel does. Full-shape replacement additionally requires distributed identified arcs and persistence on at least 90% of those arcs. Coarse relocation ranking omits the exterior path scan. These limits are heuristics evaluated on the supplied footage, not guarantees across cameras.
- A sustained partial fit at its 0.8 confidence ceiling normally compares with independent full-ROI segmentation once per 200 ms. Weaker partials permit that comparison immediately, including bounded relocation when segmentation cannot supply a reliable candidate. A relocation competing with an existing partial must yield strong full evidence; another partial cannot displace it. One resample from a relocated partial's currently observed center and common scale can recover a free shape after the coarse grid approximates its pose. This shares the existing three-profile relocation budget. A strong fresh full measurement wins even when historical size preferences give the partial fit a higher score. Accepted partial positions keep tracking alive but cannot refresh the full-shape clock or learned brightness. Current loss still produces no gaze.
- When direct rim tracking fails, estimate an intensity cutoff from a local histogram around the previous pupil, accumulating approximately its expected pixel area. Preserve that cutoff and offsets of ±6. Initial acquisition compares the dark-patch mean plus 15 with two broader patch/Otsu estimates. A moved candidate can trigger an independent dark-core comparison; weak nearby fits retain the broader recovery path. Initial weak acquisition can also try the broad Otsu cutoff. At most four threshold masks are evaluated, all inside the selected eye ROI. Manual mode still uses one absolute cutoff. A small elliptical opening removes narrow strands; its kernel follows pupil size and starts at 3 × 3 during acquisition.
- Refine mask contours using three-point inward bisectors, fit an ellipse and score contour agreement. Sample 64 positions around the fitted rim to check outward contrast. Rank candidates using that evidence, surrounding contrast, interior intensity consistency, observed dark-core similarity, a logarithmic area weight and a modest temporal preference. A confirmed pupil contributes a major-axis scale preference; the minor axis remains free to foreshorten. Core similarity and seed containment are soft preferences so a darker lash or lighting gradient does not exclude a pupil. Specular pixels brighter than the surrounding iris are excluded from core statistics only when at least half the sampled interior remains dark. A separate reflection limit, derived from the exterior intensity histogram, removes bright glints from signed rim and normal-profile measurements. Crop-edge points are excluded without discarding all remaining pupil arcs. The primitive is OpenCV’s [least-squares fitEllipse](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d3/dc0/group__imgproc__shape.html). Absolute contrast magnitude is not converted into a confidence ceiling: that rejected valid, blurred IR pupils at oblique angles. Weak recent rim measurements remain available while segmentation checks for stronger pupil evidence.
- Reflections can split a pupil into disconnected dark regions. Inspect only the eight largest shortlisted fragments, combining at most four neighboring fragments in at most three proposals per mask. Each group must lie around the dark seed or previous pupil and must pass the same rim and appearance checks. Recover weak initial dark-core arcs before broader iris masks can displace their proposal from the shortlist. This is bounded contour recovery, not a per-pixel adaptive-threshold sweep.
- For a weak fit, recover visible rim arcs using bounded deterministic stratified trials. Recovery requires at least 12 inlier points, support in 20 of 32 angular sectors, and outward contrast at 62.5% of sampled rim positions. Auto can reconstruct a pupil attached to a dark eyelid without relying on the merged region's filled area. Manual retains the additional 55% contour-inlier and 0.75–1.15 filled-area checks. If Auto still lacks an acquisition-quality fit, one blurred edge pass supplies curved arcs for another recovery attempt. Long straight edge sections and sharp reversals are excluded.
- Preserve one scale-compatible raw outline seed across the threshold masks, even when reflections make its filled-area score weak. After segmentation and robust contour recovery, a fresh normal-profile fit can refine that seed in a wider band. This requires current full-shape support; a translation-only result from a new seed is discarded. When a nearby recovery seed retains the recent major axis but substantially shrinks its minor axis, its outward brightness must persist beyond the immediate edge, with glints excluded. This conditional check helps reject an internal reflection as a new pupil boundary without restricting normal tracking or relocation. Central and annular intensity histograms detect a darker pupil inside a broad iris candidate and can select the fourth mask's cutoff. An independently darker, supported central seed can replace a textured iris seed even when the latter matches the remembered scale. A fit exceeding the confirmed major axis by more than 10%, with both a darker nested core and low interior homogeneity, is withheld as possible iris contamination. These are bounded image checks and heuristic thresholds, not pupil-identity probabilities.
- If segmentation cannot recover a recent pupil, search a bounded grid of nearby centers, then refine at most three promising rim matches. Sustained occlusion compares three common scales (0.75, 1 and 1.2) with the last accepted outline, allowing both corner shrinkage and recovery to a larger pupil. Confirmed scale checks run before coarse ranking and again after refinement, so invalid small caps cannot crowd out valid hypotheses. The coarse support gate is looser than the final measured-fit gates. Translation-only relocation must also resemble the independently observed dark pupil core; matching an iris arc geometrically is insufficient. Relocation runs after segmentation so ordinary shape changes at extreme angles can establish a new ellipse first.
- A strongly supported, established-scale pupil relocation is accepted in the same sampled frame. Initial and weaker abrupt relocations still require two-frame confirmation. Rim tracking uses observations at most 150 ms old. Position association expires after 750 ms; a shape anchor survives for three seconds after loss. Two strong full-shape observations confirm its scale. Very small and oversized replacements are rejected, while minor-axis foreshortening remains allowed. Only accepted detections update the threshold band. Strong segmentation or recovery measurements can refresh the independent shape anchor. Strong full-rim tracking also adjusts its size by 10% of the relative major-axis change, scaling both axes together and preserving the anchor's aspect ratio, center and angle. This follows gradual physiological constriction and dilation without learning shape from partial or weak fits. An unconfirmed initial scale can be corrected by new strong evidence. No current pupil means no current gaze.
- Intersect the minor-axis lines of diverse pupil ellipses. Use deterministic consensus and weighted least squares to reject outliers and unstable parallel lines. Average recent center estimates and use the observed outer pupil extent to estimate the projected sphere radius.
- Unproject each pupil center through a pinhole camera and intersect that ray with the sphere. Normalize the sphere-center-to-intersection vector to obtain gaze.

The source snapshot (with normalized line endings and trailing whitespace) and original license are in `research/`. The tutorial explicitly leaves screen calibration for a later video. Full-ROI candidate search, automatic/manual threshold controls, temporal association, the nine-point affine mapping and independent validation are robustness and usability additions to the reference pipeline. USB cameras request 640 × 480 capture. USB, network video, MJPEG and local video frames are uniformly resized to fit within 640 × 480 before pixel readback, worker transfer and ROI cropping. Aspect ratio is preserved (for example, 1920 × 1080 becomes 640 × 360), and smaller feeds are not upscaled. Saved ROI and manual corner coordinates scale to the processed dimensions. A camera may return another native capture size; network decoding and bandwidth still depend on the source stream. Detection retains its 24 fps sampling cap and one frame in flight.

The tracking/detection split and area-based intensity estimate are informed by [PuReST](https://www.hci.uni-tuebingen.de/assets/pdf/publications/TWE062018.pdf). [PuRe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.08900) informs distributed rim support, conditional fragment combination and darker nested-pupil checks. [ElSe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.06575) and [ExCuSe](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309287191_ExCuSe_Robust_Pupil_Detection_in_Real-World_Scenarios) inform the review of curved pupil arcs versus lash edges. [Świrski et al.](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/rainbow/projects/pupiltracking/) inform image-aware robust ellipse fitting at oblique angles. [Starburst](https://englab-uniud.link/2015-2016/2016-Eye-Tracker/Starburst.pdf) and the [Pupil platform paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0006) were also reviewed for edge sampling and reflection handling. A first-edge radial-ray experiment was discarded because internal glints produced unreliable boundaries. The retained code independently implements selected concepts; it does not copy the complete detectors or inherit their published accuracy or frame rates. The specific thresholds and budgets are implementation choices evaluated here. Commercial robustness also depends on optics and illumination: [Tobii describes near-infrared illumination, corneal reflections and dedicated cameras](https://www.tobii.com/resource-center/learn-articles/how-do-eye-trackers-work).

The sustained-occlusion investigation additionally reviewed the signed image support in [Świrski's paper](https://collaborative-ai.org/publications/swirski12_etra.pdf), partial-boundary fitting in [Zhu, Moore and Raphan's curvature algorithm](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/robust-pupil-center-detection-using-a-curvature-algorithm-2/), the annular image objective and temporal initialization in [Barbosa and James](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4132980/), and the distinction between visible segmentation and estimated ellipse in [CondSeg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17231). The implementation keeps bounded classical image operations; it does not introduce CondSeg's neural network. The constrained fit, learned-intensity tolerances, exterior-persistence checks and refresh cadence are implementation choices, rather than direct reproductions of these methods.

Processing remains in the worker at a maximum of 24 sampled frames per second with one frame in flight. Busy capture cycles are skipped. Changing settings or stopping/restarting a source preserves ownership of any active worker request until its response arrives; a stale response releases that request without displaying its result. Worker failure or termination also releases it. Settings changes therefore cannot enqueue additional frames behind an unfinished calculation. Grayscale conversion writes directly into a reusable ROI buffer. Successful direct rim tracking avoids full-ROI threshold/contour passes, apart from periodic shape refresh or weak-fit recovery during sustained occlusion. At most four threshold passes and one optional Canny contour pass run: the maximum is five contour passes, not four total. Relocation considers at most a 17 × 17 center grid at each of three scales during sustained occlusion, with only three profile attempts in total, including any resample from an observed partial. Each contour pass fits at most 24 shortlisted contours, each sampled to at most 256 points; each combined proposal has at most 1,024 points. At most two contour recovery attempts run per frame, each with 24 trial fits and one optional inlier refit, shared across initial-core, segmentation and edge recovery. Fresh outline recovery adds one normal-profile fit. Partial recovery uses at most 24 pairs or triples of already sampled edges. Preview masks are copied only when requested and refresh at most five times per second; their cache is keyed by method and label to avoid displaying a threshold mask as tracked edges. Processing time still depends on ROI size, image complexity and hardware.

## Mathematical conventions and deliberate corrections

Image coordinates have x to the right and y down. Camera coordinates have +z away from the camera. Ellipse `major` and `minor` are **semiaxes in pixels**, and `angle` is the major-axis angle in **radians**. OpenCV's full diameters and degree angles are converted once at the detector boundary. Crop-local positions are translated to full-frame coordinates before fitting the eye model.

For major-axis angle θ, the minor-axis direction is `(-sin θ, cos θ)`. Its perpendicular unit normal n defines a line residual `n · (c − p)`. The center minimizes the confidence-weighted sum of squared residuals. Nearly circular pupils are excluded because their orientation is indeterminate. A model becomes ready after at least 30 inliers span five of eight angular sectors. Once ready, it is retained through transient failed fits; **Rebuild model** or a relevant setting change starts a fresh fit.

For two contour-neighbor vectors a and b, the inward bisector is `a/|a| + b/|b|`. Its dot product with the vector toward the contour center is normalized by both lengths before comparison with `cos(60°) = 0.5`. The reference compared an unnormalized dot product with a cosine, which makes the decision depend on pixel scale; this implementation corrects that dimensional inconsistency.

Contour-to-ellipse error uses the first-order physical distance `abs(F) / |gradient(F)|`, where `F = (u/major)^2 + (v/minor)^2 - 1` in the ellipse's rotated coordinates. Scaling a normalized radial residual only by the minor axis understates pixel errors near the ends of an elongated pupil. Near-zero gradients return infinite error rather than manufacturing an inlier.

With vertical field of view φ and square pixels, `fx = fy = height / (2 tan(φ/2))`. The camera ray is `normalize(((u − cx)/fx, (v − cy)/fy, 1))`. The assumed eye radius R and the projected sphere's angular radius α give center distance `D = R / sin α`. This construction is exact for an on-axis spherical silhouette; representing an off-axis silhouette by an image circle remains an approximation.

For normalized ray d, origin o and sphere center C, set `b = d · (o − C)` and `c = |o − C|² − R²`. Solve `t = −b ± sqrt(b² − c)` and choose the nearest positive root. Negative discriminants and intersections behind the camera are rejected. A tiny numerical tolerance handles roundoff at tangency; the implementation does not fabricate a tangent for a missed ray. Gaze is `normalize(o + t d − C)`.

Screen features are `(gx/−gz, gy/−gz)`. An affine map fits normalized browser coordinates from nine targets using centered/scaled QR least squares, with rank and non-finite checks. Leave-one-target-out error checks calibration consistency; the five-point validation captures different targets after fitting. Predictions remain unclamped so off-screen estimates are visible as off-screen rather than falsely accurate edge hits.

## Assumptions and practical limits

The defaults of 45° vertical field of view and 12 mm eye radius are **assumptions**, not measured camera or subject parameters. Enter the actual camera field of view when known. The radius mostly sets metric scale; it cannot turn this model into a calibrated eye-anatomy measurement.

The tutorial's minor-axis convergence and outer-extent sphere fit are approximate eye models. They do not compensate for corneal refraction, lens distortion, pupil-size changes, eyelid occlusion or head/camera motion. Eye-only screen calibration is empirical and assumes a steady head and unchanged viewport. Optional head compensation uses the approximate geometry described above. A dark object can resemble a pupil, which is why careful eye-region framing and geometric fit checks matter. Real footage and camera-specific validation are needed before making accuracy claims.

## Troubleshooting threshold and pupil loss

- **Auto bias is not a fixed cutoff.** Auto primarily follows measured rim edges and adjusts segmentation when needed. Switching to **Manual** starts with the selected segmentation cutoff, or an estimated interior/exterior midpoint after rim tracking or edge recovery. The latter is a starting value for tuning; it does not reproduce the edge measurements exactly.
- A fixed cutoff does not fix the incoming camera intensities. If available, lock camera exposure/gain in the camera's own controls, focus the pupil rim, and keep illumination steady. The app crops the ROI after capture; it does not lock camera exposure. Bright reflections inside the pupil remain inside the ROI. Exposure control capabilities vary by device; see the [MediaStream Image Capture specification](https://w3c.github.io/mediacapture-image/).
- A solid mint outline is accepted. A dashed sand outline is a current, unaccepted candidate; it contributes neither eye-model observations nor gaze. Weak candidates report a short reason rather than silently disappearing. The blue sphere is an estimated eye model and is only shown from the Eye model step onward.
- Keep the pupil's full movement and some surrounding iris inside the ROI, including downward eye positions. Avoid raising the cutoff until the iris merges into it. Partial rim tracking can retain a known pupil shape while current visible arcs locate its center. This estimates the hidden part of the outline; it does not measure the occluded shape independently. Broad shadows can still resemble a pupil. Large reflections, blur, fully closed eyelids and pupils below the usable pixel size can leave insufficient information for a measurement.

The September 27 screenshot investigation reproduced a weak fit (about 74%) on the cleaner threshold mask. Rim recovery raised its geometric support to about 84%, above the unchanged 82% acquisition gate. One image-view crop also recovered; the most distorted crop still had no reliable automatic detection. These are screenshot-derived static checks, not measured gaze accuracy or proof of video stability. User image data stays out of version control.

### Remembered cameras and gaze adjustment

Camera source type, network URL, and selected USB device are stored locally per camera role. Screen eye, scene eye, outward scene, and front/head cameras keep separate selections; remote mobile, webcam, and IR selections are separate too. Returning selects the saved device but never opens it automatically. If permissions or device enumeration have not revealed a saved device, its choice remains visible so the user can reconnect or select another camera.

All live gaze views share X/Y sliders, pixel inputs, and a reset button. Positive X shifts right; positive Y shifts down. Values are normalized internally: screen/remote use viewport pixels, while scene uses the oriented scene frame. The correction is applied after mapping, leaving pupil detection and calibration samples unchanged. Validation evaluates the corrected coordinates; prior accuracy readings are cleared or labeled as previous after an adjustment. Screen and remote adjustments last only for the active calibration and reset on recalibration/reload. Only scene calibration profiles persist mappings and their offsets.
